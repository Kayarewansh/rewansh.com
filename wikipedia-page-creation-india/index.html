Why Indian Articles Get Rejected

Funding Coverage Isn't Notability.

A Legacy of Paid-Editing Scrutiny

Wikipedia's volunteer editor community has dealt with a well-documented history of undisclosed paid editing rings operating out of India, marketing founder and startup articles on behalf of PR agencies without the required disclosure. The result isn't unfair, but it is real: new India-related biography and company articles get reviewed with noticeably more skepticism for promotional tone, thin sourcing, and paid-editing red flags than a comparable article about a similar-profile subject from a market with less of that history. Going in with a genuinely notable subject, real disclosure, and neutral drafting from the start is the only way to avoid tripping those same red flags.

Why Startup Funding Writeups Usually Don't Count

Most "notable" coverage a young Indian company or founder can point to is funding-round reporting — a YourStory, Inc42, or Entrackr piece that repeats what the company's own press release said, with a founder quote layered on top. Wikipedia's guidelines treat this as too close to a primary source to establish notability on its own, because the outlet isn't independently investigating or analyzing the subject — it's relaying an announcement. A subject with five funding writeups and nothing else usually isn't ready for a Wikipedia article yet, regardless of how much total coverage that sounds like.

What Actually Counts as Independent Indian Coverage

Original reporting and features in outlets like The Economic Times, Business Standard, LiveMint, Forbes India, and The Hindu carry real weight when the piece independently analyzes, profiles, or investigates the subject rather than repeating a press release — a founder profile built on original interviews, a business-strategy feature, or investigative reporting all count. Sponsored content, guest-contributed opinion pieces, and pay-to-publish business journals do not, even when their layout makes them look identical to genuine news coverage — distinguishing the two before drafting starts is most of what a proper eligibility review actually does.

The Process

Five Steps, In This Order.

Step What Happens
1. Eligibility Review Existing coverage is mapped against Wikipedia's notability guidelines, with funding-announcement writeups specifically screened out from what counts. If the bar isn't met yet, you get told that directly.
2. Source Collection & Validation Genuine independent Indian and international journalism is gathered and checked against Wikipedia's reliability standards — sponsored placements are excluded before they become a problem.
3. Neutral Drafting The article is written in Wikipedia's neutral point of view, cited to validated sources — not promotional copy repurposed from a pitch deck or press kit.
4. Disclosed Submission via AfC Submitted through Wikipedia's own Articles for Creation review queue, with any paid involvement disclosed on-wiki. Reviewer decisions typically take 15-30 days.
5. Post-Publication Monitoring 30 days of monitoring after publication for edits, tagging, or deletion discussions — Wikipedia pages are never permanently "done."

Who This Is For

For Indian Founders and Companies With Real Coverage Beyond Funding News.

This works for Indian founders, executives, and companies who already have — or are close to having — genuine independent journalism: a founder profile, investigative business coverage, industry recognition, or international press, not just a stack of funding-round writeups.

It is not a fit for anyone whose only coverage is press-release-driven startup news. If that's the current state, the honest answer is a roadmap toward real notability, not a submission likely to be declined.
